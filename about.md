## Artist Bio

I am a papermaker, printmaker, and book artist working within material and conceptual connections to trees. I work with natural bast fibers like kozo to create delicate, thin sheets of paper. I use light as an illustrative and physical tool in tandem with my papermaking practice. When held up to light, these thin sheets of paper reveal patterns and imagery. The care and attention to tactility during the processing of fiber creates a sense of understanding of the plant. I use the raw abilities of materials with which I create work as an influence on my final product. I use etchings and mezzotints to visually illustrate the experiences that I have amongst trees. This illustrative process is a combination of representing the physical space that I inhabit and the personal feelings I have with the space. I consider this to be a meditative process, allowing the illustration to develop intuitively over time. Binding these prints into a book gives me the ability to create a narrative and encourages human interaction with the object. I am inspired by the way that early manuscripts and letterpress printed books were crafted with slowness and care. Being involved in each step in a book gives the object a handcrafted aura. To be activated, a person must touch and flip through a book. Feeling the tactility of the paper creates a connection between myself and the viewer. Withholding text from the viewer in the book form allows for personal connections to be constructed outside of a set narrative. Organic forms and shapes are influential to the stylistic choices I make in my art. However, organic also refers to the potential for life and growth, which is something I wish to give the subjects of my work.
